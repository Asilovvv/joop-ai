\section{Introduction of the Proposal}

Small businesses in Kyrgyzstan --- dental and medical clinics, beauty salons, cafes, fitness studios, driving schools,
hotels and many others --- receive most of their customer requests as messages. A customer writes in WhatsApp,
Instagram Direct or Telegram to ask about a price, whether a time is free tomorrow, where the business is located, or to
book a visit. Answering these messages quickly is directly linked to revenue: a customer who does not get an answer
within minutes usually writes to the next business in the list. Yet a typical small business has one administrator who
also greets visitors, answers the phone and handles payments, and nobody is at the desk in the evening, at night or on
days off.

The economic weight of this sector is large. According to the National Statistics Committee, small and medium-sized
businesses produced 47.3\% of the gross domestic product of Kyrgyzstan in the first half of 2025, up from 39.8\% a year
earlier \parencite{akchabar2025sme}. At the same time, the country is highly connected: 6.48~million people, or 88.5\%
of the population, used the internet at the end of 2025, and Instagram alone could reach 3.90~million users
\parencite{datareportal2026kg}. Customers therefore already expect to reach a business in a messenger, at any hour.

Large language models (LLMs) have made it possible to build conversational agents that understand free-form questions
and answer in natural language \parencite{brown2020language}. Customer support is one of the first domains where
their effect has been measured in the field: in a large study of customer support agents, access to a generative AI
assistant increased the number of issues resolved per hour by 15\% on average, with the largest gains for less
experienced workers \parencite{brynjolfsson2025generative}. However, three obstacles keep such tools away from a small
business in Kyrgyzstan. First, an LLM may produce fluent but false statements \parencite{ji2023survey}; for a business
this means an invented price, a promised time that is already taken, or a service that does not exist. Second, Kyrgyz is
a low-resource language for natural language processing \parencite{joshi2020state, alekseev2024kyrgyznlp}, and
customers switch between Kyrgyz and Russian, often within one conversation. Third, existing customer-service and CRM
products are built for companies with staff who configure integrations; a salon owner needs a system that works after a
few minutes of setup.

This thesis proposes \textbf{Joop AI}: an extensible platform of AI agents that answers the customers of small
businesses in Kyrgyz and Russian in the messengers they already use, books visits and takes orders only when the
business's own data allows it, and hands the conversation to a person when it cannot help. The study follows the design
science research approach \parencite{hevner2004design, peffers2007design}: the platform is the designed artefact, and
the research evaluates whether it serves real customers correctly.

\section{Research Problem}

Small businesses in Kyrgyzstan lose customers because messages are answered late or not at all, especially outside
working hours, while general-purpose LLM chatbots cannot be trusted to answer on behalf of a business: they may invent
prices or availability, they are weaker in Kyrgyz than in Russian or English, and they do not act on the business's real
schedule, price list and stock.

The central problem of this study is therefore:

\begin{quote}
\textit{How can an LLM-based agent platform be designed so that it answers the customers of small businesses in Kyrgyz
and Russian through their messengers, performs bookings and orders only when they are valid against the business's real
data, and can be adopted by a non-technical owner without integration work?}
\end{quote}

\subsection{Research Questions}

\begin{enumerate}
	\item[\textbf{RQ1.}] Which architecture lets a language model hold the conversation while every business action it
	proposes (a booking, an order, a request for a call-back) is checked against the business's database before it
	happens?
	\item[\textbf{RQ2.}] How accurately does such an agent answer typical customer questions about prices, working hours,
	services and availability, without inventing facts that are not in the business's data?
	\item[\textbf{RQ3.}] How well does the agent understand and answer customers who write in Kyrgyz, compared with
	Russian, and what language-specific measures improve it?
	\item[\textbf{RQ4.}] Can one platform serve businesses from different sectors (clinics, salons, restaurants, hotels,
	schools and others) through configurable sector packs instead of separate products?
	\item[\textbf{RQ5.}] What effect does the agent have on a pilot business: how many customers are served outside
	working hours, how many bookings are made by the agent, and at what cost per conversation?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Objectives}

\subsubsection{General Objective}

To design, build and evaluate Joop AI, a multi-tenant platform of AI agents that automates customer service for small
businesses of Kyrgyzstan in Kyrgyz and Russian across Telegram, WhatsApp, Instagram and a web chat.

\subsubsection{Specific Objectives}

\begin{itemize}
	\item To analyse how small businesses in Kyrgyzstan receive and answer customer messages, and which questions and
	actions repeat most often.
	\item To design an agent architecture in which the model proposes and the code decides: every booking, order and
	hand-off is validated against the database and executed atomically.
	\item To implement the platform: channel connectors, the reply engine, the business's web panel (price list, staff,
	schedule, bookings, customers, cash and stock) and self-service sign-up.
	\item To make the agent bilingual: detect Kyrgyz and Russian, answer in the customer's language and keep Kyrgyz names
	of services and frequent questions.
	\item To generalise the platform to many sectors through sector packs (labels, rules, demo data and evaluation cases).
	\item To evaluate the agent on scripted conversations and in pilot businesses with explicit, measurable criteria.
\end{itemize}

\section{Significance of the Study}

The study is significant on three levels. For \textbf{small businesses}, it offers a way to answer every customer
quickly, including at night and on days off, without hiring another administrator. For \textbf{research}, it
contributes evidence about building trustworthy LLM agents for a real, narrow task in a low-resource language, where
most published work concentrates on English and on large companies. For the \textbf{local technology sector}, it shows
that a product of this kind can be built and operated in Kyrgyzstan, in the languages of its customers, at a price a
small business can pay.

\subsection{Target Audience}

\begin{itemize}
	\item Owners and administrators of small service businesses in Kyrgyzstan who want to answer customers faster.
	\item Software engineers and students building LLM-based applications, especially for Central Asian languages.
	\item Researchers in natural language processing for Kyrgyz and other Turkic languages.
	\item Entrepreneurs and investors interested in software-as-a-service products for the regional market.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Contribution of the Study}

\begin{itemize}
	\item An architecture pattern, \enquote{the model proposes, the code decides}, that separates conversation from
	business actions and makes double bookings and invented confirmations impossible by construction.
	\item An open-source implementation \parencite{asilov2026joop} with automated tests and a public evaluation set of
	scripted bilingual conversations for twenty business sectors.
	\item Measured results on answer correctness, invented facts and Kyrgyz-language replies, and observations from pilot
	businesses.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Practices the Study Seeks to Influence}

The study seeks to change how small businesses handle incoming messages: from manual answers during working hours to an
agent that answers immediately and passes to a person only what needs a person. It also seeks to influence how
developers build LLM features for businesses: grounding the model in the business's own data, validating every action in
code, and evaluating the agent on explicit test conversations before release rather than relying on impressions.

\subsection{Motivation of the Researcher}

The researcher works with small businesses in Bishkek and sees daily how customers wait for answers in WhatsApp and
Instagram and how owners lose bookings because nobody replied in time. The researcher also wants to start a company
rather than seek employment, and this thesis is the foundation of a product that can serve real customers and earn
revenue in Kyrgyzstan.

\section{Brief Description of the Methodology}

The study follows design science research \parencite{hevner2004design} in the six activities described by
\textcite{peffers2007design}: problem identification, objectives of a solution, design and development, demonstration,
evaluation and communication. The artefact is built iteratively as a Python web service with a relational database; each
change is reviewed through a pull request and covered by automated tests. Evaluation combines (1) scripted conversations
in Russian and Kyrgyz run against the real language model with automatic pass/fail checks, (2) review of Kyrgyz replies
by a native speaker, and (3) pilot deployments in real businesses, where the platform itself records the outcome of every
conversation. Chapter~\ref{Chapter3} describes the methodology in detail.

\section{Scope and Delimitations}

\begin{itemize}
	\item The study covers text customer service in Telegram, WhatsApp, Instagram Direct and a web chat; voice messages
	are transcribed and answered as text. Phone calls are out of scope.
	\item The languages are Kyrgyz and Russian. Other languages are answered on a best-effort basis and are not evaluated.
	\item The agent uses an existing commercial language model through its API; training or fine-tuning a model is out
	of scope.
	\item The platform keeps the business's own records (bookings, customers, cash, stock) but is not a fiscal cash
	register and does not issue tax receipts.
	\item Pilots are limited to a small number of businesses in Bishkek during the study period, so the field results are
	indicative rather than statistically representative of all small businesses in Kyrgyzstan.
\end{itemize}
